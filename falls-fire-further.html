Turning a strong visual case study into a defensible monitoring framework — six research extensions, each a candidate for a future interactive layer.

01

Validate severity

Calibrate dNBR against independent evidence — field Composite Burn Index observations or official burn-severity products — instead of treating generic thresholds as universally transferable.

02

Control land cover

Separate fire from agriculture and bare soil using land-cover masks, seasonal baselines, or stratified analysis, so irrigated fields and crop-cycle bare soil don’t dominate the change product.

03

Fuse SAR + optical

Use Sentinel-1 when Sentinel-2 is obscured. Pair radar change metrics with NBR/dNBR to hold temporal continuity through smoke, cloud, and winter weather.

04

Quantify recovery

Move beyond visual fading: track median dNBR, percent area above locally validated thresholds, and recovery trajectories by vegetation type or severity zone.

05

Add terrain + hydrology

Overlay slope, drainage, soils, and rainfall to find where severe vegetation loss could translate into greater runoff, erosion, or debris-flow concern.

06

Test alternative indices

Compare RdNBR, RBR, NDVI, and moisture indices — relative indices may reduce sensitivity to pre-fire vegetation density and better separate recovery from seasonal change.

WEBSITE-READY MODULE — INTERACTIVE RECOVERY EXPLORER

A date scrubber updates the map, a severity legend, and a small recovery chart together. Hovering a pixel or zone reveals pre-fire NBR, post-fire NBR, dNBR, land-cover class, and the latest recovery value.

Design principle — separate observation from interpretation

Show the index first; then explain what makes a signal plausibly fire-related. Keep the caveat next to the visual it qualifies.
