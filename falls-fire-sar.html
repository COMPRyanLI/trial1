Why SAR Matters

01Overview02Scope & Data03Analysis04Further Study05Why SAR Matters

Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) — such as Sentinel-1 C-band radar — provides all-weather, day-and-night imaging. That matters when smoke or cloud makes optical imagery hard to use during or right after a fire.

Optical NBR

Best for spectral burn response. Directly exploits the NIR–SWIR behaviour of vegetation, moisture, char, and exposed ground.

SAR change

Best as a complementary view. Sensitive to surface roughness, structure, moisture, and geometry; useful when optical observations are missing.

CONNECTION TO THE SUPPLIED SAR BACKGROUND

For flood mapping, smooth open water often produces low radar backscatter, while wind, rain, and incidence angle alter the return. The same lesson applies here: SAR is powerful because it works through cloud and darkness, but interpretation depends on acquisition geometry and surface conditions.

For burned-area mapping, ESA Fire CCI methods demonstrate an explicit SAR–optical fusion approach: Sentinel-1 supplies temporally consistent, cloud-independent observations while Sentinel-2 contributes the spectral burn indices.
