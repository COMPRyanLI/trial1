The natural-colour pair was useful for context, but the burn signature stayed ambiguous. NBR made the disturbed central-northern zone much more legible, and dNBR most clearly isolated the fire-related change.

KEY INTERPRETATION

The central-northern burn scar is the dominant fire-related feature: subtle in RGB, clearer in post-fire NBR, and most distinct in the immediate dNBR product.

RECOVERY — THE BURN SIGNAL FADES THROUGH 2025 INTO SPRING 2026

The time series describes a progressive weakening of the central burn signature, consistent with early vegetative recovery. Read it as a change trajectory, not a claim that the ecosystem is fully recovered.

IMPORTANT CONFOUNDER

Not every bright dNBR signal is fire damage. Persistent bright signatures appear in the southern-right portion of the mapped view: irrigated fields and bare soil between crop cycles can produce chronically low NBR and therefore strong dNBR-like contrasts unrelated to fire.

Recovery is uneven

A fading burn signal doesn’t mean every ecological process has returned to baseline.

Land cover matters

Agriculture, bare soil, and seasonal moisture can mimic disturbance.

Context matters

Persistent anomalies should be compared against known land use and multiple dates.
