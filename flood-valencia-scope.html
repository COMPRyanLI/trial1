STUDY AREA

Valencia and the low-lying coastal plain to the south. The interpretation centres on La Albufera, the surrounding rice-growing lowlands, and channels draining toward the coast — places where water and saturated soils create a strong spectral signal after the flood.

DATA SOURCE

Sentinel-2 Level-2A surface reflectance, via the harmonized collection in Google Earth Engine. MSI carries 13 bands; the relevant ones span visible green, near-infrared, and shortwave infrared.

PROCESSING LOGIC

Filter by area + date → screen / mask clouds → composite reflectance → compute index.

The source figures show mean composites in Earth Engine. For production, cloud and shadow handling should be documented explicitly, because residual cloud can strongly bias water indices.

The spectral logic: NDWI maps water; NIR–SWIR explains a broader signal

PRIMARY FLOOD INDEX
NDWI = (Green − NIR) / (Green + NIR)
Open water absorbs NIR strongly, so it is dark in B8 while staying more reflective in green — a positive Green–NIR contrast. Positive NDWI often indicates open water, but a universal zero threshold shouldn’t be assumed across all land covers.
BACKGROUND CONCEPT
NBR = (NIR − SWIR) / (NIR + SWIR)
Healthy vegetation has high NIR and lower SWIR; fire damage reverses that (NIR falls, SWIR rises), so NBR drops. dNBR = NBRpre − NBRpost is widely used for wildfire burn severity.
IMPORTANT INTERPRETATION RULE

Do not label flood pixels with wildfire burn-severity classes. The NBR background is useful because it explains why NIR–SWIR combinations are sensitive to vegetation and moisture. For Valencia, a moisture-oriented metric such as NDMI — or a carefully defined change index — is more defensible for flood recovery than reusing dNBR thresholds.
