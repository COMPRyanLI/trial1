Flood Mapping Valencia

Further Study

01Overview02Scope & Data03Analysis04Further Study

Turning descriptive mapping into a validated, quantitative flood-recovery analysis — presented as a roadmap of next questions, not unfinished work.

01

Reconstruct peak inundation with SAR

Radar sees through cloud and suits event-day flood mapping. Compare Sentinel-1 water extent with CEMS rapid mapping and optical Sentinel-2 scenes.

02

Shorter time steps, quantified recession

Replace broad multi-month composites with scene-level or 5–10-day summaries. Track flooded / wet area over time and estimate how fast each zone returns to baseline.

03

Compare water indices & thresholds

Benchmark NDWI against MNDWI or AWEI, and calibrate thresholds by land cover rather than assuming a universal cutoff.

04

Add moisture & vegetation recovery

Use NDMI or a documented NIR–SWIR change metric to separate open-water recession from lingering soil and canopy moisture. Keep wildfire NBR/dNBR interpretation distinct.

05

Explain spatial controls

Overlay elevation, drainage networks, impervious cover, agricultural land use, and the La Albufera hydrologic system to test why some areas stay wet longer.

06

Move from hazard to exposure

Intersect validated flood extent with roads, buildings, cropland, and municipality boundaries to quantify who and what was exposed — reporting uncertainty explicitly.

Best next deliverable

An interactive pre / post / recovery map slider with a synchronized area-over-time chart — preserving the intuitive visual story while adding quantitative evidence and visible uncertainty.

MAKE UNCERTAINTY VISIBLE

A small “what this map can and cannot tell you” panel keeps the public-facing study credible and stops index values from being read as direct flood depth or damage.

FUTURE-STUDY ITEMS ARE RESEARCH EXTENSIONS, NOT CLAIMS ALREADY ESTABLISHED BY THE UPLOADED ANALYSIS.
